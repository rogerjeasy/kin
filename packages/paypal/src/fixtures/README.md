# PayPal responses for the adapter's tests

- `order-payer-action-required.json`: recorded from the PayPal sandbox on 16 October 2026 (`GET /v2/checkout/orders/{id}` after Kin created a top-up order). The pool's sandbox email and merchant ID are replaced with placeholders.
- `order-completed.json`: recorded from the PayPal sandbox on 16 October 2026: the first top-up through Kin, approved by a sandbox buyer, captured, and read back with `GET /v2/checkout/orders/{id}`. $100.00 gross, a $3.98 fee. The payer's and the pool's names, emails and account IDs are placeholders, and `custom_id` names the fixture's circle and member.
- `payout-sandbox-2026-10-26.json`: recorded from the PayPal sandbox on 26 October 2026 through Kin's adapter: a $1.00 payout from the pool to a sandbox personal account (`created`), the same request sent again (`duplicate`: PayPal refuses a batch ID it has seen with 400 and links the batch it made), and the batch read back while its item was `PENDING` and once it was `SUCCESS` (with a $0.25 fee, and the batch itself still `PROCESSING`). The receiver's address is a placeholder.
- `invoice-sandbox-2026-11-08.json`: recorded from the PayPal sandbox on 8 November 2026 through Kin's adapter (`pnpm --filter @kin/paypal run sandbox:check invoice …` and `invoice-read …`): the link PayPal answers a create with (`created`), its refusal of a second invoice with the same number (`duplicateNumber`, 422 `DUPLICATE_INVOICE_NUMBER`) and the search that finds the first (`search`, trimmed to the fields Kin reads), the draft, the invoice once sent, the same invoice once a sandbox personal account had paid it on PayPal's page ($166.00), and the capture its payment names (`GET /v2/payments/captures/{payment_id}`: `COMPLETED`, a $6.28 fee, `invoice_id` naming the invoice). The recipient's and the pool's emails and the pool's merchant ID are placeholders. The same run showed that a repeated `PayPal-Request-Id` does not stop Invoicing v2 making a second draft, which is why the invoice number is the idempotency key.
- `payout-venmo-sandbox-refused-2026-11-08.json`: recorded from the PayPal sandbox on 8 November 2026 by `pnpm --filter @kin/paypal run venmo:probe venmo-email --record <file>` (Kin's adapter, a $1.00 Venmo item to a sandbox personal account): the refusal PayPal gives a Venmo item, HTTP 400 `VALIDATION_ERROR`, field `items[0].recipient_wallet`, issue "Recipient wallet not supported in sandbox." The same probe run showed the identical refusal for a `USER_HANDLE` with and without `@` and for a `PHONE` in E.164, so the sandbox rejects the wallet before it reads the receiver; only the email case is kept. It also showed the PayPal-wallet control accepted (201, item `PENDING`, `recipient_wallet: "PAYPAL"`) and that a forced-failure code (`ERRPYO002` in the note) is answered 403 `SENDER_EMAIL_UNCONFIRMED` before the wallet is checked. The address is masked and PayPal's debug ID replaced.
- `payout-venmo-documented-2026-11-08.json`: **derived from PayPal's documentation, not recorded, and it cannot be re-recorded in the sandbox, which refuses every Venmo item (see the file above).** A Venmo payout (`recipient_wallet: "VENMO"`, a `PHONE` receiver in E.164, USD) written on 8 November 2026 from the recorded PayPal-wallet payout above and from the Payouts v1 documentation, with the batch once its item was `UNCLAIMED`, `SUCCESS`, `RETURNED` and `FAILED`. It shows the shape Kin expects for a Venmo payout that PayPal does accept; whether PayPal answers one exactly so is **unverified**, and no Venmo payout has succeeded in the sandbox. Sources: <https://developer.paypal.com/api/payments.payouts-batch/v1/definitions/payout_item_request>, <https://developer.paypal.com/docs/payouts/standard/integrate-api/>, <https://developer.paypal.com/docs/payouts/standard/payouts-to-venmo>. Read on 8 November 2026, the documentation says:
  - Venmo payouts are US only; every example is USD. The schema says they need a `note` and a US mobile number.
  - Item `recipient_wallet` is `PAYPAL` or `VENMO` (the guide's prose writes `Venmo`; Kin sends the enum value). `recipient_type` for Venmo is `PHONE`, `EMAIL` or `USER_HANDLE` (the schema's enum list omits `USER_HANDLE` while the item description names it).
  - Phone format disagrees between pages (`999-999-9999`, `5551232368`, `+15555550123`); Kin sends `+1` and ten digits. A handle appears once, as `@venmousername`; Kin sends it with the `@`. Whether PayPal also takes a handle without the `@` is unverified (the sandbox could not say).
  - The schema says the sandbox does not support the `PHONE` recipient type; the sandbox in fact supports no Venmo item of any recipient type.
  - A recipient with no Venmo account is sent instructions to claim the payout (`UNCLAIMED`; returned to the sender after 30 days as `RETURNED`). `FAILED` means no funds were deducted. Negative testing (`ERRPYO...` in the note) is documented only for an email item.
- `transaction-search-documented-2026-11-09.json`: **derived from PayPal's documentation, not recorded** (its sign convention is now confirmed by `transaction-search-sandbox-2026-11-11.json`). The shape of `GET /v1/reporting/transactions` (a successful payout that left the pool, with a negative `transaction_amount`, and an empty answer) and of `GET /v1/payments/payouts-item/{id}`, from the OpenAPI documents at <https://developer.paypal.com/docs/api/transaction-search/v1/schema.json> and <https://developer.paypal.com/docs/api/payments.payouts-batch/v1/schema.json>. The schema does not state the sign convention of amounts; the negative payout amount is an assumption to confirm against a sandbox payout. Emails and IDs are placeholders.
- `subscription-sandbox-2026-11-11.json`: recorded from the PayPal sandbox on 11 November 2026 through Kin's adapter by `pnpm --filter @kin/paypal run billing:record subscription|subscription-actions <id> --record <file>` (`scripts/billing-sandbox-record.ts`, which wraps the adapter's `fetch` and keeps each exchange with the request ID it carried). Replaces the two documented subscription fixtures of 10 November. Four parts, each a list of `{ method, path, requestId, request, httpStatus, body }`:
  - `create`: a 1.00 USD monthly share made three times for one circle: the circle's product under Kin's own ID `KIN-<circle id>` (201), its plan and a subscription `APPROVAL_PENDING` with its approve link; the same share again with the same key (the product refused 422 `DUPLICATE_RESOURCE_IDENTIFIER`, which the adapter reads as "already made"; the plan's request ID answered 200 with the first plan; **the subscription's request ID was not honoured: PayPal made a second subscription**); another share of the circle (the same product, a new plan). Then the first read back `APPROVAL_PENDING`, its transactions refused 404 `INVALID_RESOURCE_ID`, a suspend refused 404 and an activate refused 422 `SESSION_STATE_NOT_VALID` ("Buyer has not agreed to subscription").
  - `lifecycle`: another subscription, once a sandbox personal account had approved it on PayPal's page (a headless browser in a scratch script, the buyer's credentials from the root `.env`): read `ACTIVE` with its first 1.00 charge (fee 0.52), suspended (204) and read `SUSPENDED`, suspended again (422 `SUBSCRIPTION_STATUS_INVALID`, "subscription status should be active"), activated (204) and read `ACTIVE`, activated again (422), cancelled (204) and read `CANCELLED`, and an activate of the cancelled one (422 `SUBSCRIPTION_STATUS_INVALID`). This part was recorded before the adapter stopped sending `PayPal-Request-Id` on these calls, so its `requestId` fields show the IDs it sent then; PayPal's OpenAPI document lists the header on create only.
  - `revise`: a third approved subscription moved to 2.00: the subscription and its plan read back, a sibling plan made on the same product (201) and the revise (200, `plan_overridden: false`, an approve link to `/webapps/billing/subscriptions/update`), then the subscription read back still on its first plan. Two earlier shapes were refused by the sandbox the same day: a price override with no `plan_id` (400 `MISSING_REQUIRED_PARAMETER`) and a price override on the subscription's own plan (422 `OVERRIDES_ON_SAME_PLAN_NOT_ALLOWED`).
  - `activateWhileRevisionPending`: that subscription, suspended with its revision unanswered, refused an activate with 422 `SESSION_STATE_NOT_VALID`.
  Emails, payer IDs and the subscriber's name are placeholders, and PayPal's debug IDs are replaced. Subscription, plan and product IDs are the sandbox's own.
- `refund-sandbox-2026-11-11.json`: recorded from the PayPal sandbox on 11 November 2026 by `billing:record refund`: a 1.00 USD order paid with PayPal's public sandbox test Visa (captured at once, fee 0.52), 0.40 of it refunded with the REST API directly (Kin never makes a refund; the script does, and only there), the refund read back through the adapter (`COMPLETED`, `paypal_fee` `0.00`: PayPal kept the whole fee, `net_amount` 0.40, `create_time` in a `-08:00` offset) and the capture read back `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`. Confirms the shape of `refund-documented-2026-11-10.json`, which still covers what the sandbox cannot be made to send (the events, a reversal, a fee returned).
- `transaction-search-sandbox-2026-11-11.json`: recorded from the PayPal sandbox on 11 November 2026 by `billing:record search <payout item id>`: the payout item of the 5.00 demo caregiver payout the worker paid through the sandbox that day (batch `F3JKKBGQ5NHJE`, item `DA9N36VBMB3UN`, `SUCCESS`), and Transaction Search finding its transaction four hours later: event `T0001`, amount `-5.00`, fee `-0.25`, status `S`. Confirms the sign convention `leftThePool` assumes. The counterparty's email, name, phone, address and account IDs are placeholders.
- `subscription-revise-documented-2026-11-10.json` (removed 11 November 2026, replaced by the recording above): **derived from PayPal's documentation, not recorded.** The body Kin sends to `POST /v1/billing/subscriptions/{id}/revise` to price a monthly share's one regular cycle again (a plan override, `plan.billing_cycles[].pricing_scheme.fixed_price`), and the answer, shaped from the endpoint's reference page <https://developer.paypal.com/docs/api/subscriptions/v1/subscriptions-revise> read on 10 November 2026: HTTP 200 with `plan_id`, `plan_overridden` and `links`. The reference says a new price "requires the buyer's consent" but its sample shows only `"rel": "string"`; that the consent page arrives as a link with `rel: "approve"`, as it does when a subscription is created, is an **assumption** to confirm against the sandbox. IDs and tokens are placeholders.
- `subscription-suspend-documented-2026-11-10.json` (removed 11 November 2026, replaced by the recording above): **derived from PayPal's documentation, not recorded.** `POST /v1/billing/subscriptions/{id}/suspend` and `/activate` (each `204 No Content`, a `reason` in the body), the subscription read back with `GET /v1/billing/subscriptions/{id}` once `SUSPENDED` and once `ACTIVE` again, and the `422 SUBSCRIPTION_STATUS_INVALID` refusal of an activate on a subscription that cannot be resumed, from <https://developer.paypal.com/docs/api/subscriptions/v1/>. Used for the parent's wishes, which suspend a subscription while a trigger is active and resume it when she is home. Two points are **unverified**: the exact text of the 422, and that PayPal lets Kin suspend a subscription it did not create (the Subscriptions API acts for the merchant whose plan it is). IDs and amounts are placeholders.
- `refund-documented-2026-11-10.json`: **derived from PayPal's documentation, not recorded.** A `PAYMENT.CAPTURE.REFUNDED` and a `PAYMENT.CAPTURE.REVERSED` delivery (the resource a refund, `resource_type: "refund"`, whose `up` link names the capture), a full refund of the recorded $100.00 top-up in `order-completed.json` read back with `GET /v2/payments/refunds/{id}` once with PayPal keeping the whole $3.98 fee (`paypal_fee` `"0"`, as PayPal's own examples show) and once with all of it returned, a $30.00 partial refund, and the capture read back `REFUNDED` and `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`. Shaped from the Payments v2 OpenAPI document <https://developer.paypal.com/api/payments/v2/schema.json> (the `refund` schema, `seller_payable_breakdown`, and the examples of `POST /v2/payments/captures/{id}/refund` and `GET /v2/payments/refunds/{id}`) and the event list <https://developer.paypal.com/api/rest/webhooks/event-names/>, read on 10 November 2026. The schema says `paypal_fee` on a refund "might not match the PayPal fee that the payee paid when the payment was captured", and `net_amount` is the gross less that fee less any platform fee. Three points are **unverified**: the reversal's payload (PayPal documents none; its shape here copies the refund's), whether `GET /v2/payments/refunds/{id}` reads a reversal, and which part of the fee the sandbox returns. IDs are placeholders.
- `disputes-documented-2026-11-11.json`: **derived from PayPal's documentation, not recorded.** A list of disputes for one transaction (`GET /v1/customer/disputes?disputed_transaction_id=…`, the summary set of details, which names no transaction), an empty list, and the one dispute read by its ID (`GET /v1/customer/disputes/{id}`, whose `disputed_transactions` name the pool's capture of the recorded top-up in `order-completed.json`), shaped from the Customer Disputes v1 OpenAPI document <https://developer.paypal.com/api/customer-disputes/v1/schema.json> read on 11 November 2026 (`dispute_info`, `dispute`, `transaction_info`, the `status` and `dispute_reason` enums). Kin reads it through the PayPal Agent Toolkit's `list_disputes` and `get_dispute`. The buyer's message is an injection attempt, to show Kin never passes it on. Unverified: whether the sandbox app has the disputes scope, and that a sandbox dispute reads exactly so. IDs and names are placeholders.
